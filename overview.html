Overview

The Gaussian Random Field (GRF) module brings spatially distributed ice-sheet data onto a shared support. Ice-sheet datasets rarely agree on a grid: a regional climate model delivers monthly runoff on a 5 km mesh, an optical satellite product maps supraglacial lakes at tens of metres, an altimeter samples along irregular ground tracks. Anything that compares them, or uses them together as predictors, has to put them on common ground first.

The module fits a Gaussian random field to each snapshot of each variable and returns the fitted field, its uncertainty, and summaries of both on a regular grid.

Scientific background

Interpolating or regressing onto a shared grid ties the length scale of the result to the spacing of the input points: dense observations produce fine-scale variation and sparse ones produce smooth fields, whether or not the underlying quantity behaves that way. A Gaussian random field instead models the correlation between two locations explicitly as a function of the distance between them, so the length scale is a property of the fitted field rather than of the sampling. The fit is Bayesian, so every estimated value carries a posterior spread that later steps can propagate.

The fit runs through R-INLA. A Matérn covariance is specified as a stochastic partial differential equation, which INLA represents in finite-element form over a triangular mesh and solves by approximate Bayesian inference (Lindgren, Rue and Lindström, 2011, doi:10.1111/j.1467-9868.2011.00777.x). The model is fitted with a Gaussian likelihood and penalised-complexity priors on the range and on the marginal standard deviation of the field.

The mesh is not built here. It ships with the module as a stored R object - one is included, covering the Watson catchment in west Greenland with 295 vertices - and the same mesh is used for every variable and every timestep, so that anything fitted through this module lands on identical nodes and can be compared, swapped or combined. Observations lying outside it are dropped before fitting, since INLA refuses a fit otherwise.

Two properties of the output are worth drawing out. The spatial mean is reported with a spread taken from joint draws of the posterior rather than by combining per-node spreads: a fitted field is smooth, so neighbouring nodes err together, and treating them as independent understates the uncertainty on an average. And a timestep in which a variable takes the same value everywhere has no spatial pattern to fit; rather than failing, the module can write that constant across the field, so a record keeps its full length for anything downstream that expects regular sampling.

Repository structure

grf_module_entrypoint.py

Command-line entry point. Takes a mesh name, an input GeoZarr path and an output path, fits every timestep in the input, and writes the result. Grid resolution defaults to 100 × 100.

grf_module/grf_module.py

The fit itself, and the handling of timesteps with no spatial variation.

grf_module/spde_mesh.py

Loads a stored mesh and exposes its vertices and triangles.

grf_module/gridding.py

Interpolates fitted values from the mesh onto a regular grid, using the barycentric weights of the triangle each cell falls in.

Data flow

  1. Read the input GeoZarr and take one timestep at a time, flattening gridded data to a point cloud and discarding points outside the mesh.

  2. Fit the Matérn SPDE model on the mesh through R-INLA, or, where a variable has no spatial variation at that timestep, write out its constant.

  3. Interpolate the fitted field and its uncertainty onto a regular grid, and write all timesteps to a GeoZarr store.

For each input variable the output carries four things: the posterior mean field, its posterior standard deviation, the posterior mean of the model’s intercept, and the posterior standard deviation of the spatial mean, estimated from 500 joint draws.

Two limits are worth stating. The hyperparameters are held at their posterior mode, which is what keeps a run fast enough to repeat over a long record, but it means the reported spread reflects the field given those values rather than the uncertainty in them. And the spatial mean is taken over the whole mesh, which extends beyond any particular catchment outline; restricting it to a catchment is the caller’s job.

Use within DTC-IS

The module is deployed in the DTC-IS environment and runs as a job through the Query API. In Use Case 1 it harmonises RACMO and MAR surface runoff and a supraglacial lake record onto one mesh of the Watson catchment, so that a climate model and a satellite product can drive the same prediction of river discharge.
